Lesson 4 — Why Did Jesus Come?

Opening Question

Have you ever used an object for something completely different than what it was made for (like using a shoe to hammer a nail, or a flat-screen TV as a sled)? What happens when you don't know the true purpose of a thing?

Core Truth

Jesus did not come to earth to give us a miserable list of rules or to test us with suffering. He came to perfectly reveal the Father's love, destroy the works of the devil, and give us an abundant, victorious life.

The Law of Purpose

If you do not know the purpose of a thing, you will abuse it or lose its benefit. For centuries, religion has confused the purpose of Jesus. Many people believe Jesus came so we could "suffer humbly," or that God uses sickness and tragedy to teach us lessons. This is completely false! Jesus made His purpose very clear: "The thief does not come except to steal, and to kill, and to destroy. I have come that they may have life, and that they may have it more abundantly" (- John 10:10). This is the ultimate "plumb line" for understanding God: if it's stealing, killing, or destroying, it's the enemy. If it brings abundant life, it's God.

Repentance is a Good Word

Jesus came to call sinners to repentance (- Matt 9:13). Sometimes "repentance" sounds like an angry, religious word, but it actually just means waking up and changing your mind! Think of the Prodigal Son. He messed up his life completely and ended up feeding pigs. But then, he "came to himself" (the light clicked on), he made a decision, and he went running home to his Father (- Luke 15:17-20). Repentance is simply getting out of the darkness and running toward the Light.

Revealing the Real Father

People often think God the Father is angry, mysterious, or throwing lightning bolts down at us. But Jesus perfectly revealed what the Father is actually like. When one of His friends asked to see the Father, Jesus replied, "He who has seen Me has seen the Father" (- John 14:9). What did Jesus do? He went around doing good and healing everyone who was oppressed (- Acts 10:38). If you want to know what God the Father is like, just look at Jesus!

Destroying the Devil's Work

The Bible tells us exactly why Jesus showed up: "For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that He might destroy the works of the devil" (- 1 John 3:8). Sickness, depression, fear, and premature death are not "lessons from God"—they are works of the enemy. God is not working with the devil; He came to destroy the devil's work in your life.

Equipping You to Do the Same

Jesus didn't just come to perform miracles and leave us helpless. He came to reproduce Himself in you! Before He left, Jesus made an incredible promise: "He who believes in Me, the works that I do he will do also; and greater works than these he will do" (- John 14:12). He has sent you into the world fully equipped with His Spirit to bring that same abundant life to your friends, your family, and your school.

▶ Truth Card

Core Truth

Jesus came to give me abundant life and completely destroy the works of the enemy in my life.

Action Point

This week, when something bad happens, I will refuse to blame God. Instead, I will declare: "Jesus came to give me abundant life, and the enemy has no place here!"

The Meaning of Repentance Matthew 9:13 — "But go and learn what this means: 'I desire mercy and not sacrifice.' For I did not come to call the righteous, but sinners, to repentance."
The Heart of the Father John 14:9 — "Jesus said to him, 'Have I been with you so long, and yet you have not known Me, Philip? He who has seen Me has seen the Father; so how can you say, "Show us the Father?"'"
Doing Good and Healing Acts 10:38 — "...how God anointed Jesus of Nazareth with the Holy Spirit and with power, who went about doing good and healing all who were oppressed by the devil, for God was with Him."
Destroying the Enemy's Work 1 John 3:8 — "He who sins is of the devil, for the devil has sinned from the beginning. For this purpose the Son of God was manifested, that He might destroy the works of the devil."
The Greater Works John 14:12 — "Most assuredly, I say to you, he who believes in Me, the works that I do he will do also; and greater works than these he will do, because I go to My Father."

Group Discussion

  1. Why do you think so many people believe God causes disasters or sickness to "teach us a lesson"? How does looking at the life of Jesus change that idea?
  2. Read John 10:10. How can this single verse act like a "filter" for understanding what is happening in your life?
  3. What does the word "repentance" usually make you think of? How does the story of the Prodigal Son change that meaning for you?
  4. Jesus said we would do the same (and greater) works than He did. What is one "work of Jesus" you want to see happen in your school, family, or city?
