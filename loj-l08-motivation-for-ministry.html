Lesson 8 — The Motivation for Ministry

Opening Question

Think of a time you did something helpful for someone, not because you had to but because you genuinely cared. How did that feel different from doing the same thing out of obligation or to look good?

Core Truth

Compassion is not an emotion; it is the motivation for ministry. Every miracle Jesus performed, every person He healed and every life He restored began with being moved with compassion. That same love, received from the Father, is the source of everything we are called to do.

Ministry Is Love in Action

True ministry is a fruit of relationship with the source of ministry. It is not a title, a building or an organisation. It is the love of God, flowing through a person who has received that love, reaching someone who needs it. "For God so loved the world that He gave" (- John 3:16). The greatest act of love is giving. Jesus came as the expression of that giving.

You cannot give what you have not experienced. If you are trying to bring people the love of God without having received and rested in that love yourself, you are working from an empty tank. Knowing that God loves you, deeply and unconditionally, is not a nice bonus to the Christian life; it is the engine of ministry (- John 5:19-20).

God is love, and love is born of God (- 1 John 4:7-8). Love seeks to deliver, heal and set people free (- Luke 4:18-19). And the word the Bible most often uses to describe that love in action is compassion.

Compassion in Action: What Jesus Did

Look at what the compassion of Jesus actually produced. This is not a passive feeling; it is a force that moves and changes things.

  • Compassion for the lost: When Jesus saw the crowds, scattered and without direction like sheep with no shepherd, He was moved with compassion and reached out (- Matt 9:36). Compassion reaches.
  • Compassion for the sick: He saw a great crowd and was moved with compassion and healed their sick (- Matt 14:14). Compassion heals.
  • Compassion for the hungry: After three days with the crowds, He said, "I do not want to send them away hungry" (- Matt 15:32). Compassion provides.
  • Compassion for the helpless: The master in the parable, moved with compassion, released a servant from an impossible debt (- Matt 18:27). Compassion forgives.
  • Compassion for the blind: Jesus had compassion and touched their eyes; immediately they received their sight (- Matt 20:34). Compassion restores.
  • Compassion for the outcast: He stretched out His hand to a man with leprosy and said, "I am willing; be cleansed" (- Mark 1:41). Compassion includes the excluded.
  • Compassion for the oppressed: He sent the delivered man home to tell his friends what the Lord had done (- Mark 5:19). Compassion sets the captives free.
  • Compassion for the uninformed: He saw the crowd and was moved with compassion, and He began to teach them (- Mark 6:34). Compassion teaches.
  • Compassion for the desperate: When a father cried out for his tormented son, Jesus responded (- Mark 9:22). Compassion responds to need.
  • Compassion for the grieving: When He saw a widow who had just lost her only son, He said, "Do not weep" and raised the boy from the dead (- Luke 7:13). Compassion raises the dead.
  • Compassion for strangers: The Good Samaritan, an outsider, stopped and gave everything to help the man in the road (- Luke 10:33). Compassion crosses barriers.
  • Compassion for the repentant: The father in the parable, seeing his son still a long way off, ran to him (- Luke 15:20). Compassion restores the broken.

The Call to the Same

This is not just a description of Jesus. It is a description of what the love of God looks like when it moves through a person. Every one of these responses was simply the Father's heart being expressed through His Son. And we are called to the same.

The gifts of the Spirit are just the love of God in action. A word of knowledge for someone is love in action. Faith for someone who cannot believe for themselves is love in action. Laying hands on someone is love in action. The motivation is always the same: compassion. Not performance, not reputation, not ministry as a career. Just the Father's heart, meeting people where they are.

"Let us not love in word or in tongue, but in deed and in truth" (- 1 John 3:17-18). That is the measure. Not what we say about ministry, but what we actually do when we encounter a person in need.

▶ Truth Card

Core Truth

Compassion is the motor behind every miracle Jesus performed. The more I receive the Father's love, the more that same compassion will move through me.

Action Point

This week, I will look for one person around me who is hurting and actually do something about it, however small. Ministry begins when I stop just feeling sorry and start moving.

Group Discussion

  1. Looking at the list of things compassion produced in Jesus' ministry, which one stands out most to you? Why do you think that is?
  2. The lesson says you cannot give what you have not experienced. Do you feel like you have truly received the love of God for yourself? What would it look like to go deeper in that?
  3. What is the difference between performing acts of service out of duty and acting out of genuine compassion? Have you experienced both? Which one actually changes people?
  4. Jesus said the same works He did, we would do also (- John 14:12). If compassion is the trigger for those works, what is one specific situation in your life right now where compassion could move you to action?
